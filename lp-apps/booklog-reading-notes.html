Start a reading note in BookLog

Keep a book and the thought you want to return to together. In BookLog 4.0.2, you can add a title, write a first note, and read it again from your bookshelf without creating an account. This guide walks through those first steps.

1. Begin with one book and one moment

From your bookshelf, tap the + button. The next screen is headed Add a book. Enter a Title. Add the Author if you know it, then use Add notes for the thought you have now. Choose Save when you are ready to put the book on your bookshelf.

You do not need to turn the first note into a review. A useful starting point is often only two or three sentences: what happened, what caught your attention, and a question you want to carry into the next chapter. Writing while the detail is still fresh is more useful than trying to recreate it later.

For example, instead of “good chapter,” try a short note such as:

The narrator avoids answering the question about the old house. Is that caution, or a clue that the story is changing direction?

That gives future-you something specific to notice when you return.

2. Give the note a small job

A note is easier to reuse when it has a purpose. Pick one of these for each entry:

  • Remember: record a scene, idea, name, or page reference in your own words.
  • Question: capture what you do not yet understand or what you want to look for next.
  • Connect: link the passage to an earlier chapter, another book, or your own experience.
  • Decide: write whether you want to continue, pause, recommend, or discuss the book.

Keep the note concrete. “The chapter turns the promise into a problem” is easier to revisit than “interesting.” If you quote a book, add just enough context to know why you saved it, and respect the book’s copyright when sharing material elsewhere.

3. Open the book when you want to return

After saving, the book appears on your bookshelf. Select its row to open its detail view and read the note with the title and author together. Before your next reading session, open the book and reread the note to recover the question or detail you wanted to remember.

Try three quick rereading questions: What did I notice? What am I still wondering? What should I pay attention to next? There is no target number of notes. The useful test is whether the note helps you pick the book back up with more context than you had before.

Keep local copies in mind

For BookLog 4.0.2, book entries, notes, search history, and settings are stored primarily on the device. Clearing BookLog’s app data in Android settings or uninstalling the app removes that local app data. Keep a separate copy of anything you need to preserve. See the BookLog privacy notice for details, and the account and data deletion page for the distinction between local data and a legacy-cloud deletion request.
